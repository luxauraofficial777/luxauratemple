Condensed-Matter Reference: Dislocation Kink-Pair Tunneling & Phonon Decoherence

Scope Note: In keeping with our focus on established, peer-reviewed condensed-matter physics and materials science, this reference covers the standard models of Petukhov–Pokrovskii (1973) dislocation kink-pair quantum tunneling, Caldeira–Leggett (1981) dissipative phonon drag, Büttiker–Landauer (1982) oscillating-barrier traversal, and Glauber coherent phonon thermal decoherence in crystalline solids. It does not model speculative spacetime metric operators or temporal drift, as mechanical strain and acoustic phonons in standard physics operate strictly within the crystal lattice and do not alter spacetime metrics or gravity.

1. Petukhov–Pokrovskii Euclidean Bounce Formulation (near σ → σP)

In a crystalline lattice with Peierls period ap and Burgers vector b, a dislocation line of effective mass per unit length md ≈ ρb2 / (4π) and line tension κd ≈ Gb2 / (4π) moves across the periodic Peierls washboard potential UP(u). When an applied shear stress σ approaches the Peierls stress σP, the dimensionless stress deficit is defined as:

ε = 1 − (σ / σP) ≪ 1

Expanding the washboard potential near the inflection point yields the universal cubic metastability potential per unit length:

ΔV(ũ) ≈ b σP [ ε ũ − (π / ap) ũ2 + (2π2 / 3ap2) ũ3 ]

In the Euclidean path-integral formulation (t → −iτ), rescaling the displacement (ũ ∝ ap ε1/2), spatial coordinate along the dislocation line (x ∝ l0 ε−1/4), and imaginary time (τ ∝ τ0 ε−1/4) yields the characteristic 5/4 power law derived by Petukhov and Pokrovskii (1973):

SB(ε) / ℏ = (S0 / ℏ) ε5/4,      Γtun(ε) = ν0 exp(−SB(ε) / ℏ)

2. Büttiker–Landauer Acoustic Modulation & Caldeira–Leggett Dissipation

Oscillating Stress Field: σ(t) = σ0 + σac cos(ωact)

When an ultrasonic stress field modulates the barrier height, the characteristic imaginary-time traversal ("bounce") duration across the barrier is:

τBL(ε) = τ0 ε−1/4,      τ0 = √[ md ap / (2π b σP) ]

For typical silicate/oxide crystal parameters (τ0 ≈ 0.53 ps) and ultrasonic frequencies (fac = 20–100 kHz), the adiabaticity parameter satisfies γBL = ωac τBL(ε) ∼ 10−7 ≪ 1. Because the acoustic period (10–50 μs) is seven orders of magnitude longer than the barrier traversal time (0.5–2 ps), the dislocation experiences the ultrasonic wave in the quasi-static (adiabatic) limit, reducing the instantaneous stress deficit at the peak of the acoustic cycle to εeff = 1 − (σ0 + σac)/σP.

Caldeira–Leggett Phonon-Drag Suppression

Coupling between the moving dislocation core and the thermal bath of lattice phonons introduces a viscous drag coefficient η (in Pa·s). In the Caldeira–Leggett (1981) nonlocal action formulation, Ohmic dissipation adds a positive correction ΔSCL to the Euclidean bounce action:

ΔSCL(ε, η) ∝ η lc(ε) Δu(ε)2 = η l0 ap2 ε3/4

At room temperature (ηhigh ∼ 5 × 10−4 Pa·s), ΔSCL / ℏ ≫ 1, which strongly suppresses macroscopic quantum tunneling and ensures that room-temperature acoustic softening (the Blaha effect) is governed by classical thermal activation and stress superposition rather than quantum tunneling. Quantum kink-pair tunneling is only physically observable at cryogenic temperatures (T ≲ 1–10 K) when ε → 0.
